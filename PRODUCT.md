# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Hermes primarily serves University of Waterloo students casually looking for
something worthwhile happening soon. They may have unexpected free time, want
to meet people, or simply feel like doing something without already knowing
what to search for.

The primary job is: "I have some free time or I feel like doing something -
show me what's actually worth checking out."

Clubs, organisers, and promoters are important secondary users, but student
discovery drives product decisions first.

## Product Purpose

Hermes is a campus discovery product that turns scattered event information
into a timely, trustworthy, browse-first feed.

It helps students quickly understand what is happening, why it is relevant,
where it is, and whether it is worth showing up for. Success means students
feel more plugged into campus, less overwhelmed by fragmented information, and
confident they found something genuinely relevant to do.

## Brand Personality

Alive, effortless, trustworthy.

Hermes should feel polished but not precious, social but not chaotic, and
useful before decorative.

Reference qualities:

- Partiful's casual, socially relevant approach to joining plans
- Spotify's confident personalisation and discovery
- Notion Calendar / Cron's calm, fast product discipline
- Airbnb's trustworthy, high-confidence browsing hierarchy

## Anti-references

Hermes should not look or feel like:

- A Waterloo administration website or generic university events portal
- A dense calendar database or club bulletin board
- A link-in-bio directory
- A neon Gen-Z social app with excessive motion
- A crypto or AI SaaS landing page with vague gradients and oversized slogans
- A transactional ticketing platform where every event feels commercial
- A startup marketing site that never becomes a useful daily product

Hermes should feel student-native without trying too hard to be cool.

## Design Principles

1. **Useful before decorative.** Prioritise clear, fast, repeatable product
   workflows over campaign-style visuals.
2. **Feed-first, not feed-only.** Make spontaneous browsing excellent while
   preserving structured filters, search, and list-first location discovery.
3. **Make decisions effortless.** Event cards should quickly communicate time,
   place, vibe, relevance, and the next action.
4. **Show provenance and context.** Surface source freshness, organiser
   identity, and location context so students can trust what they see.
5. **Social without chaos.** Make plans feel alive and easy to join without
   turning discovery into a noisy or anonymous social feed.
6. **Accessibility is part of discovery.** Every student should be able to find
   something worth doing quickly and without friction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Hermes targets WCAG 2.2 AA across full pages and complete user flows.

Core priorities:

- Respect reduced-motion preferences
- Full keyboard navigation with clear focus states for cards, filters, modals,
  and map/list toggles
- Readable contrast and states that do not rely on colour alone
- Semantic event cards that expose title, time, location, price, source, and
  actions to assistive technology
- Accessible filtering and sorting for date, category, location, and happening
  soon
- Plain-language labels rather than icon-only controls or unexplained campus
  slang
- Large mobile tap targets suitable for use while walking between classes
- Clear error and empty states that explain what happened and what to do next
- Screen-reader-friendly live updates when filtered event lists change
- A list-first fallback for every map-based discovery flow
